Everyone is biased. I am, you are, the engineer on the other side is. This isn't new, and it isn't an accusation. The question is whether your process catches it before it reaches your report.

## Who it's for

Investigators at any level, supervisors who review reports, and attorneys who want to understand how bias enters a fire investigation.

## What we cover

- Expectation bias, confirmation bias, anchoring, and premature closure
- How task-irrelevant information gets onto a scene (and into your head)
- Throw out the anchor: holding hypotheses loosely until they're tested
- Stay in your lane: knowing where your expertise stops
- Peer review and technical review that actually catches something
- Case studies from published research and my own case work

## Format

Discussion-heavy. Small groups work through a scene with deliberately planted context and compare notes at the end.

## Length

One day (8 hours).
