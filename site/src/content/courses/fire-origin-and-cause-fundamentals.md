This is the course I wish I'd had in 1998. We start at the curb and work toward the area of origin the same way you would on a real scene: exterior first, least damage to most damage, documenting as we go.

## Who it's for

New investigators, firefighters moving into investigation, insurance adjusters, and anyone who has to read a fire investigation report and understand what's in it. No prior investigation experience needed. If you've worked a fire scene in any role, you already know more than you think.

## What we cover

- Fire dynamics in plain terms: heat transfer, plumes, ventilation, flashover
- Fire patterns and what they can and can't tell you
- Scene safety and the systematic scene examination
- Arc mapping and electrical evidence at the origin
- Forming and testing hypotheses under NFPA 921
- Classifying the cause: accidental, natural, incendiary, undetermined

## Format

Classroom sessions with case photos from real scenes, followed by a burn cell or acquired-structure exercise when the host agency can provide one.

## Length

Three days (24 hours). Meets the knowledge requirements in NFPA 1033 for origin and cause.
