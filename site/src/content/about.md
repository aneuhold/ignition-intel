Both have been my job for most of my 30 years in the fire service. I still work fire and explosion cases, and I still teach.

I joined the Canby Fire District as a volunteer in 1996 and went to work for Portland Fire & Rescue in 1998. There I was a firefighter, a fire training specialist, a fire inspector, and then, from 2007 to 2024, a sworn Fire/Arson Investigator and police officer.

I've worked more than 3,500 fire and explosion investigations: houses, businesses, vehicles, wildland edges, and the hard ones with injuries and fatalities. I've worked private cases since 2014, and I'm a Principal Consultant at Envista Forensics, where I've been since 2019.

For the record, the only police-like class I could get into in 1992 was Fire Investigation 101, and it got canceled. I didn't take the hint.

## Why I teach

I trained Portland's new fire investigators for years, taught as an instructor and partner at Arete Investigations, and hold instructor certifications from both the IAAI and NAFI. The lesson I come back to most wasn't a technique. It was that confidence is not accuracy. Every one of us, me included, is wired to see what we expect to see. The scientific method in NFPA 921 exists because of that, not in spite of it.

So my courses lean on the method, on documentation, and on honest testimony. Sometimes the data gets you to an origin and a cause. Sometimes it gets you to "undetermined." Both are scientific answers.
