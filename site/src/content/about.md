I investigate fires, and I teach the people who investigate them. I've done both for most of my 30 years in the fire service, and I still do both.

## Where I've worked

The only police-like class I could get into in 1992 was Fire Investigation 101. It got canceled. I joined the fire service instead.

I spent 26 years with Portland Fire & Rescue as a firefighter, fire training specialist, and fire inspector. From 2007 to 2024, I was a sworn Fire/Arson Investigator and police officer, and I trained Portland's new fire investigators. Before and alongside that, I volunteered for 17 years with Canby Fire District as a firefighter, engineer, and lieutenant.

I've worked more than 3,500 fire and explosion investigations: houses, businesses, vehicles, wildland edges, fires with injuries and fatalities, and fire-related criminal cases. In 2013 I became an instructor and partner at Arete Investigations. I've done private fire investigations since 2014, and I've been a Principal Consultant at Envista Forensics since 2019. I'm a licensed private investigator in Oregon, Washington, Alaska, and Montana, a licensed drone operator, and I do Matterport 3-D scans.

## Service to the profession

I validated three fire investigation textbooks for IFSTA: *Fire Investigator* (2nd and 3rd editions) and *Introduction to Fire Origin and Cause* (2nd edition). I sit on the Eastern Kentucky University Fire and Safety Advisory Board and the Board of Directors of the Forensic Fire Analysis Institute, and I'm a Director of the Oregon Chapter of the IAAI. I served as an IAAI Director from 2013 to 2018.

The Oregon Chapter of the IAAI and IAAI International both named me Fire Investigator of the Year in 2022.

## Why I teach

I've seen good investigators reach the wrong answer because they trusted a feeling over a process. I've done it myself. What I teach is the habit of testing what you think against what the scene shows, and the honesty to write "undetermined" when that's where the evidence stops.
