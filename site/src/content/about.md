I'm Nicole Brewer. I investigate fires, and I teach the people who investigate them. I've done both for most of my career. Today I'm a Principal Consultant at Envista Forensics, and I teach origin and cause, the scientific method, documentation, and testimony.

I've worked more than 3,500 fire and explosion investigations: houses, businesses, vehicles, wildland edges, fires with injuries and fatalities, greater alarms, and the criminal cases that followed some of them. I've done private investigations since 2014 and joined Envista in 2019. I'm a licensed private investigator in Oregon, Washington, Alaska, and Montana, and a licensed drone operator.

Most of that came out of 30 years in the fire service. Seventeen of them were as a volunteer firefighter, engineer, and lieutenant with Canby Fire District. Twenty-six were with Portland Fire & Rescue, as a firefighter, fire training specialist, and fire inspector, then for 17 years as a sworn Fire/Arson Investigator and police officer, until I left the department in 2024.

The teaching runs alongside all of it. I was a fire training specialist at Portland, and I trained the new fire investigators who came up behind me. In 2013 I became an instructor and partner at Arete Investigations. I'm an IAAI Certified Instructor and a NAFI Certified Fire Investigation Instructor.

I didn't take a straight line here. The only police-like class I could get into in 1992 was Fire Investigation 101, and it got canceled. (The earliest award on my record is still a coloring contest from the 80's.) I took the hint and kept going.

I teach because the scene is a hard place to learn. Most of what I know I learned from people who were willing to show me their work, mistakes included. Confidence is not accuracy, and "undetermined" is a scientific answer. If you leave one of my classes believing both, it went well.
